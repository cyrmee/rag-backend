Project Zephyr Field Notes

Project Zephyr is an internal codename for a weather-balloon telemetry relay
built by the Aeris Research Group. The relay hardware uses a custom
low-power radio module called the Corvid-9 transceiver, which operates on
the 433 MHz ISM band and draws only 18 milliamps in transmit mode.

The lead engineer on Project Zephyr is Dr. Wren Okafor, who joined the Aeris
Research Group in 2019 after completing postdoctoral work in atmospheric
physics. Okafor designed the Corvid-9's power management circuit specifically
to survive temperatures as low as minus 60 degrees Celsius at high altitude.

Field testing for Project Zephyr took place in the Kestrel Valley test range
during the autumn of 2023. Across fourteen balloon launches, the Corvid-9
transceiver maintained an uninterrupted telemetry link for an average of
11.4 hours per flight, with the longest single flight lasting 19 hours before
the balloon's envelope ruptured over the Salt Flats.

The project's total budget was 2.4 million dollars, funded jointly by the
Aeris Research Group and a grant from the National Atmospheric Research
Consortium. The next milestone for Project Zephyr is a satellite handoff
protocol, allowing the Corvid-9 to relay data through low-earth-orbit
relays once it drifts beyond ground-station range.
